# Faithful Word Music — Service Planning Philosophy

## Purpose

The primary goal of the song service is to get the congregation to **sing out loudly, participate confidently, and enjoy the service**.

Because of that, the song list should primarily emphasize songs that the congregation already knows well and enjoys singing.

Familiar and beloved songs should form the backbone of normal service planning.

This does not mean that new, less familiar, or rarely used songs should be avoided. Those songs are still important and should continue to be introduced and used. However, they should be added carefully so that the overall service remains familiar, energetic, and easy for the congregation to participate in.

The general principle is:

> Favor strong, familiar congregational songs while intentionally and gradually introducing less familiar material.

These guidelines are intended to guide planning rather than create unnecessary rigid rules unless a rule is specifically described as required.

---

## Familiarity and Song Rotation

### Familiar Songs

Songs that are well known, well loved, and sung confidently by the congregation should make up most of a normal service.

The AI should not avoid a good familiar song simply because it has been used before. Familiarity is a positive quality in congregational singing.

At the same time, the overall song library should still be used intelligently so that the same small group of songs is not used constantly when other suitable songs are available.

Historical usage may provide evidence that a song is familiar, but it does **not** by itself prove that a song is loved or preferred by the congregation. The AI should not claim that a song is beloved, popular, or especially well liked unless the system has data that actually supports that conclusion.

### Completely New Songs

A completely new song should **not normally be introduced more than once in a week**.

In most circumstances, introducing a new song approximately **once every other week** is preferable.

This is a general pacing guideline rather than a requirement to introduce a new song on a fixed schedule. There may be weeks where no new song should be introduced.

### Less Familiar Songs

Songs that have been sung before but have not been used recently, or songs that the congregation does not yet know especially well, can be used more frequently than completely new songs.

Generally, a service week may include approximately **one to three less-familiar songs total**, depending on the circumstances.

This does not mean repeating the same less-familiar song in multiple services. It means that different less-familiar songs may appear throughout the week's services while the majority of the overall song list remains familiar.

For example, it may be reasonable for Sunday morning, Sunday evening, and Wednesday evening each to contain one less-familiar song, provided the rest of the songs are strong and familiar and none of those less-familiar songs are themselves being overused.

The AI should always consider the balance between:

- congregation familiarity
- recent usage
- song quality and suitability
- how many unfamiliar songs are already present in the surrounding services

---

## Inserts

By the time the AI is helping build a service plan, the week's **insert will already have been selected**.

The insert should therefore be treated as a **fixed part of the service plan**, not as something the AI needs to choose or replace during normal plan generation.

If the Service Planner already provides the insert's exact position in the service, the AI should also treat that position as fixed unless a future feature explicitly allows changing it.

The same insert is sung at all three regular services in the week:

- Sunday morning
- Sunday evening
- Wednesday evening

Because the insert is repeated across all three services, the AI should **not** build all three services around the exact same theme simply because the insert is present in each one.

### Connecting the Insert to the Service

When possible, the surrounding song service should loosely connect with the insert's subject, mood, or place in the overall progression of the service.

The insert may act as one part of the service's story or thematic movement.

However, this connection should remain flexible.

The AI should not force an unnatural theme around the insert when the connection is weak or when doing so would make the service repetitive.

The general principle is:

> When it fits naturally, let the service lead into, out of, or otherwise complement the insert. When it does not fit naturally, do not force it.

### Avoiding Weekly Redundancy

Because the same insert appears in all three services, each service should still maintain its own identity.

For example, if the insert is about the resurrection, that does not mean Sunday morning, Sunday evening, and Wednesday evening should all become nearly identical resurrection-themed services.

One service may connect to the insert more directly, while another may connect through a broader related idea, and another may simply place the insert naturally within a different progression.

The AI should consider the week's three services together and avoid producing three lists that feel like variations of the same list.

The repeated insert is expected.

The rest of the service should still provide variety.

---

## Service Flow

A good song service should have a sense of progression.

The songs do not need to follow one strict theme, but they should generally feel as though they belong together and can loosely tell a story or move through related ideas.

Thematic connection should be treated as a **guiding principle, not a rigid formula**.

A service should normally feel coherent without becoming repetitive.

When an insert is present, the service's progression should take the insert into account where it can do so naturally.

---

## The Opener

The first song of the service is the **opener**.

The opener is especially important because it sets the mood for the song service.

A strong opener should generally:

- immediately engage the congregation
- be familiar enough to encourage strong participation
- establish the initial mood, direction, or broad theme of the service
- work naturally as the beginning of the service's overall progression

The opener may also begin the loose thematic story that the remaining songs develop.

The first two songs may be more closely connected thematically than the rest of the service.

When useful, the opener and early portion of the service may also begin a progression that eventually connects naturally with the predetermined insert.

However, the opener should not be forced into the insert's theme merely because the insert is present.

---

## The Middle of the Service

The remaining songs should generally continue naturally from the opener without forcing the entire service into one narrow theme.

Some songs may be closely connected to each other while others may only share a broader spiritual or emotional connection.

The AI should avoid constructing a song service where every song says essentially the same thing.

Variety is important.

A good service may move naturally between related subjects while still feeling cohesive.

The goal is **connection without redundancy**.

If the insert fits naturally into that progression, the AI should take advantage of that connection.

---

## Songs That Should Not Be Directly Paired

Songs that differ too strongly in both **thematic meaning and musical character** should not be placed directly next to each other when the transition would feel unnatural.

For example:

- **"Onward, Christian Soldiers"**
- **"Cleanse Me"**

should not be directly paired together.

"Onward, Christian Soldiers" has a strong, triumphant, militant character, while "Cleanse Me" is much more reflective, personal, and introspective. Moving directly from one to the other would create an abrupt change in both message and overall character.

This does not mean that strongly contrasting songs can never appear in the same service.

The important distinction is **direct pairing**.

If enough musical, thematic, or structural space exists between contrasting songs, both may still work within the same service.

The AI should consider:

- lyrical and thematic meaning
- explicitly available information about mood, style, or musical character

when evaluating whether two songs transition naturally into one another.

The AI must not invent unsupported musical analysis. If the system does not contain grounded information about a song's musical style or character, the AI should rely only on information it can actually support.

The same direct-pairing principle applies to the songs placed immediately before and after an insert.

---

## The Closer

The final congregational song is the **closer**.

The closer occurs after the sermon and immediately before dismissal.

Because a full sermon occurs between the earlier song service and the closer, the closer does not need to maintain as strong a thematic connection to the earlier songs or to the insert.

The closer may therefore stray further from the service's earlier theme or progression.

A closer is often:

- shorter
- simple
- familiar
- appropriate for ending the service
- thematically suitable for dismissal or conclusion

However, the closer does **not** have to be short. A longer song may still be used when it works well as the final song.

The main consideration is whether the song functions naturally as a conclusion to the service.

---

## Avoiding Over-Theming

Normal services should **not** be planned around an excessively strict single theme.

The service may begin with a clear direction and should have reasonable continuity, but repeatedly selecting songs that communicate almost exactly the same message can make the service feel redundant.

The AI should prefer a natural progression of ideas rather than simply searching for several songs containing the same theme or keywords.

The strongest thematic connection will often occur between the opener and the songs immediately following it.

Later songs may branch into related ideas while still maintaining reasonable continuity.

A predetermined insert does not override this principle.

The AI should not take the insert's subject and make every other song in the service about exactly the same thing.

---

## Planning Across the Week

Sunday morning, Sunday evening, and Wednesday evening should be considered both individually and as parts of the same service week.

Each service should feel internally coherent, but the three services should not feel unnecessarily repetitive when compared with one another.

The AI should consider:

- songs already used earlier in the week
- the insert that appears in all three services
- recurring themes
- familiarity
- less-familiar material
- the general mood and progression of each service

The goal is to make each service strong on its own while maintaining variety across the week.

The fact that the same insert is sung three times is **not** a reason to repeat the same thematic structure three times.

---

## Seasonal Planning

Seasonal services are important exceptions to the normal rules regarding repetition and thematic variety.

### Thanksgiving Week

Thanksgiving Day is always on Thursday.

The **Sunday and Wednesday immediately before Thanksgiving** should emphasize Thanksgiving-related songs.

Because the available Thanksgiving repertoire is limited, thematic repetition and repeated use of certain songs during this period is acceptable.

The AI should still make the best possible balance between:

- well-loved Thanksgiving songs
- less familiar Thanksgiving songs
- recent usage
- the limited size of the Thanksgiving repertoire

Normal expectations about avoiding redundancy should be relaxed during this period.

### Christmas Season

Every service **on or after Thanksgiving and through Christmas Day** must use **only Christmas songs**.

This is a **hard seasonal rule**.

During this period, normal concerns about thematic redundancy are significantly relaxed because the available Christmas repertoire is limited and the entire season is intentionally focused on Christmas music.

The AI should still make intelligent choices within the Christmas repertoire.

It should continue to consider:

- congregation familiarity
- beloved and popular Christmas songs when supported by actual data
- less familiar Christmas songs
- recent usage
- variety between services
- good service flow

However, repetition during this season is much more acceptable than during the rest of the year.

Any predetermined inserts during the Christmas season should remain within the Christmas repertoire and should still be considered when building the flow of each service.

### Easter

Easter is always observed on Sunday.

The **week leading up to Easter** should emphasize songs concerning the **death and crucifixion of Jesus Christ**.

On **Easter Sunday**, the service should emphasize songs concerning the **resurrection of Jesus Christ**.

Easter Sunday may be much more tightly themed than a normal Sunday because the resurrection is intentionally the focus of the service.

The normal rule against excessive thematic repetition should therefore be relaxed for Easter.

---

## How the AI Should Apply These Guidelines

These guidelines describe the Music Director's actual planning philosophy.

The AI should use them when:

- recommending songs
- evaluating proposed song lists
- comparing possible songs
- suggesting replacements
- eventually generating service plans

The AI should combine this philosophy with available factual information such as:

- song lyrics
- song history
- recent usage
- upcoming plans
- predetermined inserts
- explicit familiarity or preference data, if such data exists
- grounded information about song mood or musical character, if such data exists

Historical usage alone does not prove that a song is loved or familiar. The AI should not make unsupported claims about congregation preference.

Likewise, lyrics alone do not establish every aspect of musical style or character. The AI should not present unsupported musical judgments as grounded facts.

The AI should distinguish between:

1. **Hard rules stated in this document**
2. **General planning preferences stated in this document**
3. **Its own recommendation or judgment**

If this document does not establish a specific rule, the AI must not invent one and present it as Music Director policy.

For example, unless a specific minimum number of days is established elsewhere, the AI should not claim that a song "cannot be repeated within 60 days."

Instead, it may explain that a song was used recently and recommend a less recently used alternative based on the broader preference for variety.

---

## General Principle

When multiple technically valid song choices exist, favor the option that is most likely to produce a **strong, confident, enjoyable congregational song service**.

Familiarity is generally more valuable than novelty.

Variety is valuable, but it should not come at the expense of congregational participation.

New and less-familiar songs should continue to be introduced, but they should be surrounded by songs the congregation already knows and enjoys.

Predetermined inserts should be incorporated naturally into each service when possible, but they should not dictate the entire week's theme.

The goal is not simply to create an interesting list on paper.

The goal is to create a song service that the congregation will actually **sing**.
